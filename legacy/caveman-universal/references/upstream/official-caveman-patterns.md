# Caveman: extended phrase reference

Use this when the quick list in SKILL.md doesn't cover the phrase in front of you, or when you want more worked examples before trimming a long document.

## Throat-clearing openers

Cut these, or replace with the actual content:
- "It is important to note that..."
- "It should be noted that..."
- "It is worth mentioning that..."
- "This paper/section/report aims to explore/discuss/examine..."
- "As we all know..." / "As is well known..." — either the reader already knows it, so it's redundant, or doesn't, so it's an unsupported claim. Cut either way.
- "Needless to say..." — if it's needless to say, don't say it.
- "In today's world..." / "In this day and age..."

## Stacked hedges

Keep at most one hedge per claim; delete the rest.
- "could possibly perhaps" → "may" (or nothing, if other evidence in the sentence already establishes certainty)
- "it seems to suggest that it might indicate" → "suggests"
- "there is a possibility that it could potentially" → "it could" or "it may"

## Empty transitions

- "Moving forward,"
- "That being said," / "With that said,"
- "At the end of the day,"
- "When all is said and done,"
- "It goes without saying that..." — then don't say it.

Keep transitions that carry real logic: "however," "therefore," "because," "as a result," "in contrast." These tell the reader how two ideas relate to each other, which is information, not decoration.

## Nominalizations

Verbs smothered into noun phrases:

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| conduct an analysis of | analyze |
| make a decision on | decide |
| perform an evaluation of | evaluate |
| provide assistance to | help |
| give consideration to | consider |
| carry out an investigation into | investigate |
| reach a conclusion about | conclude |
| put emphasis on | emphasize |
| make an announcement | announce |
| have a discussion about | discuss |

## Vague qualifiers standing in for a known fact

Only cut these if a real fact is available to replace them — otherwise the vagueness is honest, not lazy.
- "a significant number of" → the actual count or percentage, if known
- "in the near future" → the actual date or timeframe, if known
- "a variety of factors" → name the factors, if known
- "many experts believe" → name the experts or source, if known. If not, this is a real (if weak) hedge — don't delete it and state the claim as settled fact.

## Redundant pairs and doubled modifiers

each and every · full and complete · first and foremost · various different · each individual · basic fundamentals · past history · future plans · end result · final outcome · completely eliminate · absolutely essential · totally unique · close proximity · advance planning · unexpected surprise · new innovation

Pick one word from each pair.

## Padding passive voice

Flip to active only when the actor is known and relevant:
- "The decision was made by management to..." → "Management decided to..."
- "It was found by the researchers that..." → "The researchers found..."

Leave passive alone when:
- the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally de-emphasized ("the samples were collected in triplicate")
- the field's convention is passive (much of the natural sciences, some legal writing)

## Writing-about-the-writing

- "As mentioned previously,"
- "As will be discussed in the next section,"
- "This section will now turn to..."
- "It is the purpose of this paper to..."

A genuine cross-reference that helps navigation in a long document (e.g., "(see Section 3.2)") is fine — it's the throat-clearing *around* it that's the problem, not the pointer itself.

## Verbal-tic intensifiers

very · really · quite · extremely · incredibly · basically · essentially · actually · in fact · truly · definitely — when used as a reflex rather than to add real emphasis.

If the emphasis is genuine, upgrade to one stronger word instead of deleting outright: "very important" → "critical" · "really big" → "major."

## More worked examples

### Business email

**Input:**
> Hi team, I just wanted to reach out and touch base regarding the upcoming deadline. I think it's important that we all get on the same page about this. Moving forward, I would really appreciate it if everyone could try to submit their reports by Friday, if that's at all possible. Thanks so much in advance, really appreciate it!

**Output:**
> Hi team — please submit your reports by Friday. Thanks!

### Report summary

**Input:**
> The purpose of this report is to provide an overview and summary of the findings of the study that was conducted. It was determined by the analysis that there were a number of significant factors that were found to contribute to the overall delay in the process, and it is worth noting that these factors should be taken into consideration going forward.

**Output:**
> The analysis found several factors driving the delay: [list them from the source data]. [State the recommendation, if any.]

The brackets matter here: the source text never names the factors or gives a count, so the trimmed version doesn't invent one — it marks exactly where the real content belongs. Cutting padding is about making room for substance, not papering over a gap with a plausible-sounding number.

### Academic-style paragraph

**Input:**
> It is widely acknowledged in the literature that supply chain delays can arise from a multitude of different causes. In this particular study, it was found that the majority of the delays that occurred could be attributed primarily to gaps in the handover process between shifts, which is consistent with what has been noted in prior research on this topic.

**Output:**
> Supply chain delays have many possible causes. In this study, most delays traced to gaps in shift handover — consistent with prior research.
